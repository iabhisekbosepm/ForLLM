# Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy — Reusable Template

> **How to use this template**
> A GTM strategy is the documented plan for how you bring a product to a defined market: *who* you're selling to, *why they'll care*, *how* you reach and convert them, and *how you'll know it worked*.
> - **PART A — Core**: the 9 components every GTM needs. Fill these for any launch.
> - **PART B — Extended**: deeper sections for big launches, new-market entry, or board/investor review.
>
> **The one rule that governs everything:** **Get the ICP right first.** Positioning, channels, pricing, and enablement all derive from it — get the ICP wrong and the rest of this document is fiction. Validate it with **10+ real conversations** with people who match it before committing the plan.
>
> **Other best-practice rules**
> - **Pick the sales motion by deal size:** PLG/self-serve (low ACV) → hybrid (mid) → sales-led (high ACV).
> - **Start with 2–3 channels you have conviction in** — where the ICP already spends time, not what's cheapest. Don't spread thin.
> - **GTM fails on coordination, not ideas.** Keep channels and cross-team feedback loops (product ↔ marketing ↔ sales ↔ support) tight.
> - **Living document.** Review at 30/60/90 days post-launch and iterate.
> - If a section doesn't apply, write "N/A — because…". Delete this block and italic *prompts* once written.

## B1. Market Sizing & Opportunity

- Each persona: goals, pains, role in the purchase, objections.

- Campaign themes, content calendar, SEO/paid/organic mix.

- GTM budget by channel/activity.
- Team/headcount and external vendors.

- Channel/reseller/integration partners and their role in distribution.
