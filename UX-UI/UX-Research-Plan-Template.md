# UX Research Plan Template — Reusable

> **How to use this template**
> Two tiers in one place:
> - **PART A — Core Research Plan**: Required for every research initiative. Always fill this.
> - **PART B — Extended Methodologies**: Add for specialized studies (diary, contextual inquiry, survey, etc.).
>
> **Rules of thumb**
> - Define the decision before the research. "What product decision will this research inform?" If you can't answer that, stop — the research isn't ready.
> - Methodology follows the question. Never "we always do interviews" — match method to research question type.
> - Saturation over sample size. For explorative research, stop when new interviews yield < 10% new insights (~8–12 interviews/segment).
> - Split collection and synthesis. Synthesize after every 4–5 sessions — don't batch 20 interviews then analyze.
> - Delete this instruction block and italic *guiding prompts* once filled.
>
> **Methodology selection guide**
> | Question type | Method | Timeline | Cost |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | "Can they do X?" | Moderated usability test | 2–3 weeks | $2K–5K |
> | "Why do they do X?" | In-depth interviews | 2–3 weeks | $1K–2K |
> | "How often does X happen?" | Survey / Diary study | 1–3 weeks | $300–2K |
> | "What do they call X?" | Card sort / Tree test | 1 week | $500–1K |
> | "X in real context?" | Contextual inquiry | 4–6 weeks | $3K–8K |

# PART B — EXTENDED METHODOLOGIES
*(Add the relevant section based on your chosen method.)*

## B1. Moderated Usability Test Protocol

*Use when: "Can they do X?" — seeing real-time blockers and workarounds.*

**Session structure (45–60 min):**
1. Welcome + consent (5 min)
2. Warm-up questions (5 min)
3. Task scenarios (30–35 min): 4–6 tasks max
4. Post-task debrief (5 min)
5. SUS survey (5 min)
6. Closing questions (5 min)

**Task scenario format:**
```
Context: [Realistic background — who the user is, what they're trying to accomplish]
Task: [Open-ended goal — never mention UI elements]
Success criteria: [Observable behavior that confirms task completion]
```

**Facilitation rules:**
- Think-aloud protocol: "Please tell me what you're thinking as you go"
- No leading: Never confirm they're doing it right
- Non-directive prompts: "What are you thinking?" not "Why did you click there?"
- Wait 8–10 seconds after asking before rephrasing

---

## B2. In-Depth Interview Protocol

*Use when: "Why do they do X?" — exploring motivation and decision-making.*

**Question sequence:**
1. Context building (8–10 min): Role, day-in-life, environment
2. Problem exploration (20–25 min): Past behavior, not future intentions
3. Current solution exploration (10–12 min): Workarounds, tools, friction
4. Decision factors (5–8 min): What would make them switch?
5. Close (2–3 min): What did I miss? Who else should I talk to?

**Mom Test principle:** Ask about past behavior, never future intentions.
- Right: "Tell me about the last time you encountered this."
- Wrong: "Would you use a product that did X?"

---

## B3. Contextual Inquiry Protocol

*Use when: Observing users in their real environment to catch what they don't mention.*

**Session structure (2–4 hours on-site or remote screen share):**
1. Observe user doing their actual work (silent observer — don't help)
2. Note: tools used, where they get stuck, what they ask colleagues, emotional cues
3. Debrief (30 min): "I saw you switch to [tool] — why?"

**Note-taking during observation:**
- Facts: Specific behaviors, tools, timeline, numbers
- Quotes: Exact verbatim (in quotes)
- Contradictions: Anything unexpected or surprising
- Questions: Mark with ??? for debrief

---

## B4. Diary Study Protocol

*Use when: Tracking behavior over days or weeks — frequency, context, mood.*

- **Entry frequency:** Daily (more data, higher dropout) or 3×/week (balance)
- **Entry format:** Voice memo (easiest) > short form (structured) > long text (highest effort)
- **Duration:** 7–14 days
- **Prompts (2–3 per entry):**
  - "What task did you attempt? Succeed or fail?"
  - "What tool did you use? Why?"
  - "What frustrated you, if anything?"
- **Dropout:** Expect 20–30% dropout; recruit 25% more than target
- **Incentive:** $100–200 for full study (higher than one-off sessions)

---

## B5. Survey Design

*Use when: Validating breadth of a finding found in qualitative research.*

**Sample size formula (proportion validation):**
```
n = (Z² × p × (1−p)) / E²
Z = 1.96 (95% confidence) | p = 0.5 (if unknown) | E = 0.05 (±5% margin)
Result: n ≈ 385 responses minimum
```

**Question format rules:**
- Use open-ended for "what" and "why" (not yes/no)
- Use Likert scale (5–7 points) for degree/agreement
- Max 10 questions — every question must link to a research question
- Never: Multiple questions in one, leading language, hypotheticals ("Would you pay $X?")

---

## B6. Card Sort & Tree Test

*Use when: Designing or validating information architecture and navigation.*

**Open card sort** (discovery): Users group and name categories themselves
- Sample: 15–30 participants
- Output: Frequency tables, affinity diagram
- When: Before designing IA

**Closed card sort** (validation): Users sort into pre-defined categories
- Sample: 20–40 participants
- Success metric: > 75% placement accuracy per category
- When: After IA draft to test fit

**Tree test** (navigation validation): Text-based tree + task-based questions
- Sample: 20–40 participants
- Success metric: > 75% task completion rate
- When: Before design, after IA draft

---

## B7. Synthesis Framework

*Run this after every 4–5 sessions — not as a batch at the end.*

1. **Extract** 1–3 behavioral insights per session (separate sticky notes — one per note)
2. **Cluster** by theme: Jobs / Pain Points / Current Solutions / Blockers / Success Criteria
3. **Count frequency:** How many sessions mention each cluster?
4. **Flag outliers:** Single-mention surprises (often contain the best insights)
5. **Generate implications:** "If X is true, then we should…"

**Saturation signal:** Stop recruiting when last 2–3 sessions yield < 10% new insights.
